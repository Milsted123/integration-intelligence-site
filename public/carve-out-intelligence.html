A different kind of risk

Six things that make a carve out different from an integration

The tools buyers use to assess integration readiness don't translate to the seller's side. Carve-Out Intelligence was built from scratch around the five structural differences that shape every disposal.

01

The perimeter is drawn, not given

Acquiring a business means acquiring a legal entity. Separating one means drawing a boundary across legal entities, assets, contracts, technology, data, and people. That boundary is rarely clear at the start; perimeter ambiguity is the single most common reason carve outs underdeliver or get repriced in due diligence.

02

Two stories, not one

Every carve out has two businesses that need a credible standalone narrative: the one leaving and the one left behind. RemainCo capability erosion is one of the most underestimated risks in any disposal; the hidden dependencies, informal knowledge, and customer relationships that quietly go with the business.

03

Day 1 is a hard deadline

In an integration, Day 1 is the start of the programme. In a carve out, it is the programme. Everything the separated business needs to operate must exist at close, with no post close build window, no parent to fall back on, and no scope for a phased handover the buyer has not already agreed to in the TSA.

04

Three cost categories, routinely underestimated

Dissynergies, one-time separation costs, and stranded costs sit in structurally different parts of the P&L and are routinely confused with each other. Getting them wrong affects the proceeds, the buyer's confidence in the numbers, and the seller's negotiating position.

05

TSA design and exit are as consequential as the deal

Transitional Service Agreements are consequential. Duration, scope, and exit credibility are among the most consequential decisions in a carve out; a TSA that runs too long erodes CarvedCo independence; one that runs too short collapses at exit. The exit plan matters as much as the agreement itself.

06

The buyer will find what you haven't

Every gap in your separation story is a repricing opportunity in due diligence. Carve-Out Intelligence works the way a rigorous buyer would, systematically across the same dimensions they will interrogate, so the gaps surface before the process does.

The pipeline

Same rigour as Integration Intelligence. Built for the seller's side.

Carve-Out Intelligence runs the same four-stage analytical pipeline, Intake through to Output, with a library, failure patterns, and advisory output written entirely around carve-out and separation dynamics.

Stage 01 · Intake

Document extraction

Deal documents, separation feasibility studies, TSA drafts, and carve out financials are processed into a structured risk signal payload. Whatever you have, we work with it.

Stage 02 · Engine

Pattern matching

Eight archetypes and 24 failure patterns are evaluated against your deal. Compound risk states, where two patterns compound rather than simply add, are identified separately.

Stage 03 · Advisory

Seller side analysis

Two outputs: a Strategic Risk Assessment (O1) for the board, and an Execution Blueprint (O2) for the programme team. Both are written from the seller's perspective throughout, framing risk as the seller's execution risk.

Stage 04 · Output

Board report and brief

A board level report and a management brief, typically within a week of documents landing. The portal surfaces the five questions the board should be asking before the deal moves forward.
